Buying Guide: 3 Steps to Choosing Yours

Check for double-sided material. This lets you hang the projector on either side depending on your yard layout.
Look for grommets, not just a plain sheet. Grommets make tensioning the screen with rope or bungees much easier and reduce sag.
Plan your ambient light. Portable fabric screens perform best after dusk — full daylight will wash out the image regardless of screen quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this screen be used outdoors in a backyard?

Yes, the double-sided fabric works for both indoor and outdoor projection, and it packs down flat for easy setup on a fence, wall, or portable frame.

How does the double-sided design help?

You can project from the front or rear, which is useful outdoors when you want to avoid the projector beam crossing where guests are sitting.

Does it need a special frame to hang?

No frame required — it comes with grommets so you can hang it from a fence, tree branches, or a basic PVC frame.

What size backyard is 120 inches good for?

A 120-inch screen comfortably fits 15-25 viewers in a typical backyard setup, seated 12-18 feet back.

Does the fabric wrinkle after folding?

Light creases can appear after storage, but they mostly relax once the screen is hung taut, and a quick steam or hang-dry removes stubborn ones.
