How to choose a dog harness in 4 questions

1. Does your dog pull?

Then you want a front clip on the chest. When the dog pulls, the leash turns them gently back toward you instead of giving them something to lean into. A back clip is more comfortable for dogs that already walk nicely, for jogging and for long hikes. Most vest harnesses here have both.

2. Vest or strap harness?

A vest spreads pressure over a wider padded area and usually has a handle on top, good for big dogs and for helping them into the car. A strap harness like the PetSafe Easy Walk is lighter and cooler, but has less padding and no handle.

3. Over the head or step-free?

Some dogs hate having anything pulled over their head. Look for a harness with a buckle at the neck, like the PHOEPET, so you can put it on without going over the ears.

4. Is your dog an escape artist?

Dogs with narrow heads or deep chests (greyhounds, whippets, some shepherds) can back out of a normal harness. A third strap around the belly, like on the rabbitgoo Escape-Proof, makes that much harder.

How to measure your dog for a harness

Measure the widest part of the chest, just behind the front legs, with a soft tape. Then measure the neck where a collar sits. Compare both numbers with the brand's size chart, not with the dog's breed or weight. If your dog falls between two sizes, most owners choose the larger one and tighten it.

A good fit passes the two-finger test: you should be able to slide two fingers flat under any strap, but not more. Too loose and the dog can slip out; too tight and it will rub behind the front legs. Check the fit again after a few walks, because straps loosen.

Harness or collar?

A collar is fine for holding ID tags and for dogs that walk calmly. For dogs that pull, lunge or have breathing or neck problems (pugs, bulldogs, small breeds with fragile tracheas), a harness moves the pressure from the throat to the chest. Many owners use both: a collar for tags at home and a harness for walks.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best dog harness vest for pulling?

A vest with a front clip, like the rabbitgoo No-Pull or the Ruffwear Front Range. Clip the leash to the chest ring: when the dog pulls, it is turned back toward you. If you prefer a light strap harness, the PetSafe Easy Walk is the classic front-clip option.

Are no-pull harnesses bad for dogs?

A well-fitted front-clip harness is widely used by trainers. The concern is fit: straps that cross the front of the shoulders too low can limit movement. Choose a Y-shaped vest that leaves the shoulders free and check the fit often.

Can a dog wear a harness all day?

It is better to take it off at home. Constant wear can cause rubbing and matted fur under the straps. Put it on for walks, car rides and training.

Which harness is best for a dog that slips out?

One with a third strap around the belly, like the rabbitgoo Escape-Proof. Also check the fit: most escapes happen because the harness is too loose.

Should I clip the leash to the front or the back?

Front for dogs that pull or while training; back for dogs that already walk well, for running and for hiking. Harnesses with both clips let you switch as your dog improves.

How do I wash a dog harness?

Most fabric harnesses can be hand-washed with mild soap and air-dried. Check the label: some can go in the washing machine inside a laundry bag. Do not put them in the dryer, which can weaken buckles and reflective strips.
