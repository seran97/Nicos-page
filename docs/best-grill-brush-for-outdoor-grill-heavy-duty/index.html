Buying guide: 3 things to check first

  • Check bristle attachment quality, not just bristle material — Poorly attached bristles are the real safety issue, not metal itself.
  • Match handle length to your grill setup — Longer handles matter most for larger grills or deep fireboxes.
  • Replace the brush at the first sign of fraying — Don't wait for bristles to fully come loose before swapping it out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do metal bristles shed onto the grates?

This model's bristle design is built to minimize shedding, but as with any wire brush, it's worth doing a quick visual check of grates before cooking, especially as the brush ages.

Is 30.5 inches too long for a small grill?

The long handle is mainly there to keep your hand away from heat, so it works fine even on smaller grills — you simply won't need the full reach.

How often should I replace the brush?

Most owners replace wire grill brushes every season or once bristles visibly start to fray, which is standard practice regardless of brand.

Does it work on porcelain-coated grates?

Yes, though some owners with porcelain grates prefer a slightly gentler pass to avoid scratching the coating over time.

Is the scraper attachment removable?

The scraper is built into the brush head design rather than a separate detachable piece, which keeps it simpler but means the whole head wears together.
