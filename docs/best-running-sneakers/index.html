How to choose a running shoe in 4 questions

1. How far and how often do you run?

For a few easy runs a week, any of the daily trainers here works. If you run long distances or more than four days a week, more cushioning (Gel-Nimbus 27, Clifton 10) protects your legs on tired days.

2. Do you land on your heel or midfoot?

Most recreational runners land on the heel. A higher drop (10 to 12 mm, like the Ghost 17 or Pegasus 41) reduces calf and Achilles load. Midfoot strikers often prefer 6 to 8 mm (880v15, Ride 18, Nimbus 27).

3. Soft or responsive?

Soft shoes feel great on easy days but can feel slow. Firmer, livelier shoes (Pegasus 41, Ride 18) feel better when you speed up. If you only want one pair, pick something in the middle.

4. What shape is your foot?

Fit matters more than any feature. If standard shoes squeeze your toes, look for a wide version (New Balance offers several widths). Leave about a thumb's width in front of your longest toe.

Running shoes vs. fashion sneakers

A fashion sneaker can look like a running shoe and still feel fine for a walk. The difference shows up after a few kilometers: running shoes use foams designed to absorb repeated impact and to keep their shape for hundreds of miles, plus outsoles placed where runners wear them down. If you run regularly, buy a running shoe for running and keep the lifestyle pair for everything else.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best running sneaker for beginners?

A neutral, well-cushioned daily trainer is the safest start. In this guide that is the Brooks Ghost 17 or the Hoka Clifton 10. Avoid racing shoes and very minimal shoes until you run regularly without pain.

Are running shoes and jogging sneakers the same thing?

Yes. Jogging is easy running, and the same daily trainers work for both. The difference that matters is not the name but cushioning, drop and fit for your foot.

What does heel-to-toe drop mean?

Drop is the height difference between the heel and the forefoot, in millimeters. A higher drop (10 to 12 mm) takes load off the calves and Achilles and suits heel strikers. A lower drop (4 to 6 mm) loads the calves more and suits midfoot strikers. If you change drop by a lot, do it gradually.

Should I size up for running shoes?

Many runners go half a size up from their casual shoes because feet swell during runs. Leave about a thumb's width between your longest toe and the end of the shoe, and check the brand's size chart before ordering.

How often should I replace running shoes?

A common guideline is every 300 to 500 miles (about 500 to 800 km). Replace them sooner if the foam feels flat, the outsole is worn through or new aches appear after runs.

Do I need a stability shoe?

Only if your feet roll inward a lot (overpronation) and that causes discomfort. Every shoe in this guide is neutral. Most runners do well in a neutral shoe; if you had injuries linked to overpronation, ask a specialist or try a stability version such as the Brooks Adrenaline or ASICS Gel-Kayano.
