How to Check If Your Stroller Has an Active Safety Recall

Stroller recalls are more common than most parents realize, and they can be issued years after a product was purchased, often for hazards like wheel detachment, hinge collapse, or entrapment points that only surface after widespread use. Knowing exactly where to check, and how to get notified automatically, matters more than remembering to look it up once at purchase.

What a Recall Actually Means

In the United States, a product recall is coordinated through the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC), typically after the agency and a manufacturer agree that a product poses a substantial risk of injury under normal or foreseeable use. Recalls can range from a free repair kit to a full refund or replacement, depending on the severity and nature of the defect.

A recall is not necessarily an indication that a product is unsafe in every unit — sometimes only a specific manufacturing date range, batch, or model variant is affected. That's exactly why checking the specific model and, where applicable, the manufacture date on your particular stroller matters more than just checking the general product line.

Searching the Official CPSC Recall Database

The CPSC maintains a public, searchable recall database at cpsc.gov/Recalls, which can be filtered by product category (such as 'strollers' under juvenile products), date range, or keyword. Each recall listing includes the hazard description, the remedy being offered, and instructions for how to claim it, which is useful even if you're not certain of the exact model name.

The CPSC also operates SaferProducts.gov, a public database where consumers can search incident reports and file a report themselves if a product causes or almost causes an injury. Checking both isn't redundant — the recall database shows official actions, while incident reports can surface an emerging pattern before a formal recall is issued.

Registering Your Stroller for Direct Notices

Under the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act, manufacturers of durable infant and toddler products, including strollers, are required to provide a postage-paid registration card with the product and to maintain a system for registering purchasers, precisely so they can send a direct notice if a recall is later issued. Filling this out, either by mail or through the manufacturer's online registration page, means you don't have to remember to check manually at all.

If the original registration card was lost, most manufacturers still allow online registration using the model number and serial number found on a sticker on the stroller frame, usually near the rear axle or under the seat. It's worth doing this even for a stroller bought secondhand or received as a gift, since registration is tied to the product, not the original purchaser.

What to Do If Your Stroller Is Recalled

If a search turns up an active recall matching your model, the listing will specify whether to stop using the product immediately or whether it's safe to keep using with caution until a fix is applied — this distinction matters and is stated explicitly in each recall notice. Contact information for the remedy (a replacement part, a repair kit, or a refund) is included directly in the CPSC listing and on the manufacturer's own site.

After resolving an active recall, it's worth periodically rechecking the database every few months for the life of the product, since new information about a hazard can emerge well after a stroller has been in regular use, and recalls are sometimes expanded to cover additional model years or variants after the initial notice.

FAQ

Where is the official place to check for a stroller recall in the United States?
The CPSC's public recall database at cpsc.gov/Recalls is the official source, and it can be searched or filtered by product category, including strollers.
Does a stroller recall mean the product is banned or must be destroyed?
No. Most recalls offer a remedy such as a free repair kit, replacement part, or refund, and the recall notice specifies whether continued use is safe in the meantime or should stop immediately.
Why should I register a stroller with the manufacturer if I already know how to check for recalls online?
Registration allows the manufacturer to send a direct notice if a recall is issued, rather than relying on you to periodically search the database yourself.
What information do I need to register a secondhand stroller?
Typically the model number and serial number, both usually found on a sticker on the frame, are enough to complete registration even without the original purchase receipt.
