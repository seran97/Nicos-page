Buying Guide

Confirm it's a true broad-spectrum SPF. Look for "broad spectrum" on the label, not just an SPF number, to ensure UVA and UVB coverage.
Check the tint against your undertone. Read reviews from people with a similar skin tone before committing, since mineral tints vary in how they oxidize.
Apply enough product. Most people under-apply sunscreen — a nickel-sized amount for the face is the standard dermatologist recommendation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a tinted SPF 50 replace my regular foundation?

For many people it can on low-key days — it gives light, even coverage — but if you need full glam coverage, layer your usual foundation lightly on top.

Will the tint match my skin tone?

Mineral tinted formulas like this one usually adapt somewhat to your undertone, but it's worth checking swatch photos or reviews from people with a similar skin tone before buying.

Is mineral sunscreen actually effective at SPF 50?

Yes, mineral (physical) sunscreens using zinc oxide or titanium dioxide can absolutely reach SPF 50 and above; the key is applying enough product and reapplying every two hours in direct sun.

Can I use this daily under makeup?

Yes, it's designed as a primer-sunscreen hybrid, so it sits well under makeup and can become a daily step before you apply concealer or powder.

Is it suitable for oily or acne-prone skin?

Mineral formulas tend to be gentler on breakout-prone skin than chemical filters, and the pore-blurring effect is a bonus, but always patch test first if you're acne-prone.
