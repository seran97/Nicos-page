How to pick a serum in 4 steps

1. Start from one goal, not five

Pick the problem that bothers you most: breakouts and oil (niacinamide, BHA), texture and fine lines (retinol), dullness and dark spots (vitamin C) or dryness (hyaluronic acid). One well-chosen serum beats a crowded routine.

2. Match strength to your skin

Higher percentages are not always better. A 20% vitamin C or a retinol can irritate sensitive skin. If you are new to an active, start with a gentle formula two or three times a week.

3. Know what not to combine

Use retinol at night and vitamin C in the morning. Avoid stacking retinol and strong acids such as BHA on the same night when you are starting out. Niacinamide and hyaluronic acid pair well with almost everything.

4. Always finish with sunscreen

Retinol, acids and vitamin C make the benefits of daily sunscreen even more important. See our SPF 50 face sunscreen guide.

A simple routine that works

Morning: gentle cleanser, vitamin C or niacinamide, moisturizer, sunscreen.
Night: cleanser, then retinol or BHA (alternate nights at first), hyaluronic acid if your skin feels tight, moisturizer.

Introduce one new active at a time and give it 6 to 12 weeks before you judge it. For a full routine by skin type see our skin care routine guide.

Frequently asked questions

Which face serum is best for improving skin texture?

For rough texture and clogged pores, a 2% BHA such as Paula's Choice works fastest. For texture plus fine lines and post-acne marks over time, a retinol such as CeraVe Resurfacing Retinol Serum is the usual choice.

Can I use vitamin C and retinol together?

Yes, but not at the same time. Use vitamin C in the morning and retinol at night to reduce irritation.

Can I use niacinamide with vitamin C?

Yes. Modern formulas can be layered. If your skin flushes, use them at different times of day.

How long do serums take to work?

Hydration is immediate. Oil control and texture often improve in 4 to 8 weeks. Dark spots and fine lines usually need about 12 weeks of consistent use.

Do I need more than one serum?

No. Most people do well with one targeted serum plus moisturizer and sunscreen. Add a second only when the first is part of your routine without irritation.

Are cheap serums as good as expensive ones?

Often, yes. What matters is the active ingredient, its concentration and a stable formula. Several of the picks here cost a fraction of luxury brands with similar actives.
