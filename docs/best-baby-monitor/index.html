How to choose a baby monitor in 4 questions

1. WiFi or no WiFi?

No-WiFi monitors link the camera directly to a parent screen. They work during internet outages and are not exposed online. WiFi monitors add phone viewing and features like sleep tracking, but depend on your internet and need a strong password and updates.

2. Do you need to watch from outside the house?

If not, a no-WiFi monitor is simpler. If you want to check in from work or a grandparent's house, you need WiFi or a hybrid model.

3. How big is your home?

Direct-connection monitors list ranges up to about 1,000 feet in open air; walls cut that a lot. Test the signal in the rooms you will actually use within the return window.

4. Battery and night use

A parent unit with long battery life lets you carry it around. Look for night vision, a sound-activated (VOX) mode to save battery, and temperature display if your nursery gets hot or cold.

Safe sleep comes first

The American Academy of Pediatrics says home cardiorespiratory monitors should not be used as a strategy to reduce the risk of SIDS, and that consumer sleep monitors should not replace safe sleep practices. A monitor helps you hear and see your baby; it does not make sleep safer by itself.

  • Place the baby on their back, on a firm, flat surface, in their own crib or bassinet.
  • Keep pillows, blankets, bumpers and toys out of the sleep space.
  • Mount the camera and run its cord at least 3 feet from the crib, out of the baby's reach.

Keeping a WiFi monitor private

  • Change the default password and use a long, unique one.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication in the app if it is offered.
  • Install firmware and app updates.
  • Use a separate guest network for smart-home devices if your router supports it.

Frequently asked questions

Is a no-WiFi baby monitor safer?

For privacy, yes: it cannot be reached over the internet. It also keeps working when the internet is down. The trade-off is that you cannot watch from your phone away from home.

Do I need a video monitor or is audio enough?

Audio is enough for many families, especially in small homes. Video helps you check whether the baby is awake without opening the door, and is useful once the baby moves around the crib.

How far away should the camera be from the crib?

Keep the camera and its cord at least 3 feet away from the crib so the baby cannot reach them.

Can a baby monitor prevent SIDS?

No. The AAP advises against relying on monitors to reduce SIDS risk. Safe sleep practices are what lower the risk.

How long will I use a baby monitor?

Many families use one until age 2 to 4, while the child sleeps in a crib or until they can come to you on their own.
